*— Journal entry, written 12 January 2024, 5:00 a.m.*

On a winter night like any other, in the small hours, lying in bed after a shower, I suddenly realised: my life seems to have stopped on some afternoon three or five years ago.

Back then, I think I was writing an FFT template (honestly, mostly copied from other people's solutions), pleased with myself for getting my own code to run, and wondering whether I should go back and find out what a Fourier Transform actually is. Then I thought, no need. It was all for showing off anyway, so why put real effort into it? I went off to write my blog instead. Today's subtitle can be this line: "My once upon a time is over."

Now, it seems, I am learning the Fourier Transform again. Unlike last time, this time there is an exam, so I have to get it (btw, I still haven't). Compared with back then, even my coding skills are getting worse.

It feels as if, after a certain point, every stage repeats something from the stage before, only appearing, and existing, in another form.

Maybe, maybe, I will still remember that early spring, going to the park next to Jinjiang Wanda. Back then, I would think of a noon in the dorm two years before, when I went from a boy to an angsty, melodramatic teenager, and my dreams broke along with my hopes. Just like me now, out of nowhere, suddenly thinking of the person I used to be, and then going from a child to a man, just as childish, and yet stronger.

What has broken will never come back together. Only forgetting stops it from shining so brightly, because a shattered mirror can still throw back a blinding light. Forgetting? Sort of. When I talk about the past now, it no longer carries any strong feeling. It is like telling someone else's story, and all of it can be let go. With a lot of things, once I give them meaning myself, I end up with less drive; it is encouragement from other people that becomes the strongest fuel.

"My once upon a time is over."

It is as if, in the shower, I enter another space, where my thoughts float freely through the universe, drifting, never stopping. In truth, I haven't been in that space for a long time. Since when? To protect myself better, I stopped talking to myself.

When I stopped keeping a diary back then, it was to make peace with myself, make peace with the past, and walk towards the future. Picking up the pen again this time means picking the past back up, and facing the future in a new way.

I really love this line. It feels like saying goodbye to my past self without completely leaving: a "see you", not a "never see you again". What has ended, or rather what is beginning again, is not youth, and certainly not life, but those moments: the person writing a blog five years ago, the person in the park three years ago, or the person on a winter morning writing a new journal entry, and a new chapter of his life.
